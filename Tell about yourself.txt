In Oracle, "MRP" stands for "Managed Recovery Process" and "RFS" stands for "Remote File Server"; both are background processes used in Oracle Data Guard, where MRP applies archived redo logs to the standby database, while RFS receives those archived redo logs from the primary database and writes them to the standby redo logs on the standby server. 
Key points about MRP and RFS:
RFS Function:
The RFS process runs on the standby database and is responsible for receiving redo data (archived redo logs) transmitted from the primary database over the network and writing it to the standby redo logs.
MRP Function:
The MRP process reads the redo data from the standby redo logs received by RFS and applies it to the standby database, essentially keeping the standby database synchronized with the primary. 
How they work together:
Data Transfer:
When changes occur on the primary database, redo logs are generated and archived. 
RFS Role:
The RFS process on the standby database receives these archived redo logs from the primary. 
MRP Role:
The MRP process then reads the received redo data from the standby redo logs and applies it to the standby database, effectively making the standby a copy of the primary.
